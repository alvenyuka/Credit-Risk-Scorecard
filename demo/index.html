A scorecard that gives every decline a reason.

A points-based credit scorecard built on 307,511 Home Credit applications across eight relational tables: Weight of Evidence bins coarse-classed so every bin's points move one way, a logistic regression written from first principles and checked against scikit-learn, and 600 points calibrated to 20:1 good-to-bad odds. Tested once on 61,503 applicants it had never seen.

    Reason codes

    Every decline names what cost the points.

    Each characteristic contributes points. For a declined applicant, the reasons are the characteristics where the applicant's bin sits furthest below that characteristic's best bin. This is a real applicant from the holdout.

    Under the Equal Credit Opportunity Act and Regulation B a lender must state the principal reasons for an adverse action. The points gap gives that statement directly, without a separate explanation model.

    Fairness and regulation

    What was left out on purpose.

    Sex and marital status are prohibited bases under ECOA and Regulation B, so they were removed before fitting. The repository measures what that exclusion cost rather than assuming it was free.

    Prohibited bases excluded

    The information value of each excluded characteristic is reported so the decision is auditable: both were weak predictors, and the AUC moves by about a thousandth.

    Age 62 and over

    Regulation B lets age be used only if applicants aged 62 or over are not scored less favourably. Age did not survive feature selection, so the check passes by construction and the test asserts it.

    Limits

    What this result does not show.

    Stated so a reader can weigh the figures rather than take them on trust.

    • The holdout is a random, stratified 20% of applicants, not a later period, so the figures measure discrimination within one vintage, not stability through a downturn. The population-stability index between training and holdout scores is reported as a sanity check only.
    • Credit loss uses an assumed 45% loss given default and the application's credit amount as exposure. A lender's own recovery data would change the money figures, though not the ranking.
    • Home Credit is one lender in one market, and the dataset's currency is not stated. The policy table is a method demonstration, not a Kenyan pricing exercise.
    • The three external bureau scores carry most of the signal. Where a lender lacks bureau coverage, as many Kenyan SACCOs do, the card would need rebuilding on the remaining characteristics and its AUC would fall.
